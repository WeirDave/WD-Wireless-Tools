# The requirement area can be drawn on the plan, in Prep and in Capacity

Suite **2.231.0**. Prep **2.7.0**. Capacity **1.12.0**. Every other tool is unchanged.

## What changed

### Prep (Requirement areas stage)

- **A floor's requirement area can be drawn on the plan** instead of taking the one worked out from the walls. Open the **Requirement areas** stage, pick the floor, and use **Draw the area** in the right-hand panel:
  - **Draw a rectangle** drags one out on the plan.
  - **Draw a shape** takes a click for each corner. Clicking the first corner, double-clicking or pressing **Enter** finishes it; **Backspace** takes the last corner back; **Esc** cancels.
  - A drawn area can be changed: drag a corner to move it, or the small dot in the middle of an edge to add a corner.
  - **Back to automatic** takes the drawing away from that floor.
- **The floor strip marks a drawn floor "Your area"**, and the floor's row in the panel gives its size in feet, marked as drawn.
- **A drawn area is written instead of the computed outline**, on that floor only. It goes into the area already on the floor when there is one, keeping that area's name and requirement, and becomes a new area when there is not.
- **A drawing follows the trim.** It is drawn on the plan as it is opened, and when the trim crops that floor the outline moves with the crop. A corner outside the cropped sheet is held on its edge, and the floor's row says so.
- **A floor that is left alone does not use a drawing.** A floor set to keep its existing devices, or set to 0 people, is not written. Its row says the drawn area is not used and, for existing devices, that one of the **Replace** choices under **Floors that already have devices** makes it apply.
- **The result line counts them**: "put a requirement area on 3 floors, 2 of them the outline you drew".

### Capacity

- **New step 2, "Draw the area"** (optional), between the template and applying it. The steps on the left are now Template, Draw the area, Apply it to this project, and What this project already has. It shows the open project's floors as tabs above their plan, with the area each would get as a dashed pink line, and the same **Draw a rectangle**, **Draw a shape** and **Back to automatic** controls as Prep, with the same corner editing and keys.
- **The preview and the file agree.** The floor cards in step 3 describe the drawn outline ("The area you drew on the plan", with its size), and **Apply** writes it. A floor with an area already drawn in Ekahau has that outline replaced by the drawn one and keeps the area's name and requirement.
- **A floor that is left alone says the drawing is not used**, with the same reason and the same remedy as in Prep.
- **The result line** reports how many floors used a drawn outline.

### Shared by both tools

- **One tool in both.** The drawing, its rules and the sentence under the buttons are a single module (`WD.AreaEditor`), so the two tools cannot differ.
- **What is refused**, in the same words on the page and in the file writer: fewer than three corners ("An area needs at least three corners."), a shape too small to be an area ("That area is too small."), and an outline that crosses itself ("The outline crosses itself."). A refused corner move is undone.
- **Dragging the plan still moves it.** A plain drag pans everywhere except on a corner or an edge dot of a drawn area, or while a rectangle or shape is being drawn. Scrolling zooms and **Fit** shows the whole sheet.

## Verified

- The drawing is driven with real mouse and keyboard events in Chrome, Edge and Firefox on both pages: a rectangle, a shape closed by a double-click, a corner moved, **Back to automatic**, **Esc**, and the buttons switched off with their reason when the stage is unticked. The outline asserted is the one sent to the server, in the floor's plan units, at the place the pointer was.
- The writer is tested for a floor with nothing drawn, with an area already on it, and with existing devices under each of the three choices, and for the trim: the outline lands where it was drawn on the cropped sheet, a corner outside it is held on the edge, and a floor the trim left alone keeps its outline.
- The page and the server are run on the same shapes and refuse the same ones in the same words.
- Removing any of these turns a test red: the writer using the drawn outline, the outline following the crop, either route passing the drawing on, either page sending it, a corner claiming the drag, and the crossing check.
- The suite passes.
