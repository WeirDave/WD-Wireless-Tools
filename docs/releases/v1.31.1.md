*"When I asked your brother about this he said around 15–18 dB for 6 GHz, lean toward 18–20 dB since 6 GHz attenuates harder through those materials."* — Dave, relaying advice from another Claude session viewing the actual .esx.

The Framery One wall type shipped in [v1.31.0](v1.31.0.md) with `attenuationFactor: 100`, which at 0.06 m thickness works out to only ~6 dB effective loss. Realistic for a cubicle, but not a soundproof pod with steel bracing, acoustic wool, and a laminated glass door. Dave's other Claude session, looking at the real project, called that out — signal was still bleeding through the pod zones as if the walls weren't there.

**Fix.** Bumped `attenuationFactor` from `100` to `300` across all three bands. At the same 0.06 m thickness that's ~18 dB effective loss, matching the "lean toward the higher end" target for 6 GHz — where WiFi attenuates hardest through composite panels and glass.

Held everything else steady:

- Thickness: `0.06 m`
- Reflection: `0.405` (steel-frame reflectivity)
- Diffraction: `11`
- Color: `#9C27B0`

Kept a single value across 2.4 / 5 / 6 GHz to match how every other wall type in *Recommended by WD* is set up — Ekahau's propagation model handles the per-band scaling internally, and using a unified value keeps the type easy to draw with and easy to tune later.

Suite `v1.31.0 → v1.31.1`. Quick Walls `v7.29 → v7.30`.
