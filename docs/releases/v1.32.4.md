## Why one wall type still fits

The whole lineup shares the same wall stack — powder-coated steel exterior + acoustic core + PET/polyester interior + laminated 8 mm sound-control glass door. Only dimensions and weight vary. So a single wall type ("Framery Boxes") remains the right modeling call — the physics is identical, the footprint is what changes.

## The under-modeling problem this fixes

The v1.31.1 value of `attenuationFactor: 300` (~18 dB effective at 0.06 m) assumed composite/acoustic-wool construction with a laminated glass door. Real construction — confirmed above — is **powder-coated sheet steel on every exterior surface**. That's a Faraday envelope with one glass door, not a soundproofed acoustic panel with a metal frame.

**Fix.** Bumped `attenuationFactor` from `300` to `500` across all three bands. At 0.06 m thickness that's ~30 dB effective — supported by:

- **Framery's own ISO 23351-1 Class A rating of 30 dB DS,A** speech reduction — acoustic and RF attenuation aren't identical, but similar mass-loaded steel construction achieving 30 dB acoustic isolation typically lands in the 25-35 dB range for Wi-Fi bands
- **Ekahau's own Elevator Shaft wall type**: 150 att × 0.2 m = 30 dB effective (the closest physical analog in the stock library — a metal-lined vertical enclosure)
- **Real-world coated/laminated glass measurements**: 20-25 dB per pane at 5 GHz ([WiFi Hotshots](https://wifihotshots.com/services/wireless/orange-county-site-survey/)), 20-40 dB range for cellular ([Metro Wireless DAS Discovery Checklist](https://www.metrowireless.com/blog/das-discovery-checklist-planning-deployment))
- **Weighted average**: 3 steel-panel sides (~35 dB) + 1 laminated glass door (~20 dB) ≈ 31 dB — hits our 30 dB target

Everything else stayed put:

- Thickness: `0.06 m` (Framery doesn't publish a wall thickness; 60 mm is consistent with the observed sandwich construction and pod weight-to-surface ratios)
- Reflection: `0.405` (steel-panel reflectivity)
- Diffraction: `11`
- Color: `#9C27B0`

## Known limitation Ekahau can't model

Framery's construction has powder-coated steel panels on the **roof and base** too, but Ekahau's 2D wall model has no ceiling-material concept — walls are floor-to-ceiling vertical planes. An AP mounted directly above a pod will always show as covering the interior in the heatmap, regardless of wall values, because the sim doesn't know there's a metal ceiling. The wall-type bump helps for lateral RF (through sidewalls and door) — for the "AP above the pod" case, only physical AP repositioning (or wiring the pod) fixes it.
