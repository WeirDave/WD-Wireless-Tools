    Quick Walls
    Quick Walls can show you the folder a project came from once it has been saved, which is otherwise awkward to find again.
    This is the template Auto-apply on open puts into a project as soon as you open one, and the one the picker in Quick Walls starts on. Until now it was not chosen at all: it silently became whichever template you last pressed Apply on, so applying Ekahau Default once to reset a project made that the template every later project was given. Pressing Apply no longer changes it — this is the only place it is set.
    The rest of the wall template controls stay in Quick Walls, on the Template bar above the wall list, because the decision is made while looking at the template: the picker, Apply, Save Template, Manage and the Auto-apply on open tick box. What is saved of them is listed below.

    Backup & restore
     
    Every setting on this page, your wall templates — which is where your Quick Walls keyboard shortcuts live — your capacity templates, Squirrel's rules, PlanTrim's saved boxes and your report details. Your saved Ekahau login is never in it.
    Two things it is for: getting back what you had after something overwrites it, and copying your setup to another machine.
    Importing shows you exactly what would change before it writes, and copies your current settings aside first.
    Panel widths, which sections you left folded and tips you have dismissed stay with the browser you set them in. They are in the file so you can read it, but a restore skips them — carrying the work machine's layout onto this one would be a nuisance rather than a rescue. Dark/light and your Ekahau sharing group do come back, because those follow you rather than the window.
    Before an update, and before an import, a copy of your settings is written to ~/.wd_wireless_tools/settings-backups/ without being asked for. The newest three of each kind are kept and older ones are removed automatically. Settings are the only thing in the suite copied aside like this — an import has to land on settings.json itself, so there is nothing else to fall back on. Projects work the other way: every tool that derives one writes it under a new name, leaving your original untouched.
